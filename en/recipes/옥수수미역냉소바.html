#CornSeaweedColdSoba

2026-07-12 · 28°C/cloudy · 300kcal

Notes

20260712, #BreakfastDiary 1777, 28°C/cloudy Use ingredients A, B, and C to make today's breakfast; if C is left over, tomorrow's breakfast builds around C plus D and E. The day after, use whatever combination of C, D, and E makes sense. Isn't that a genuinely practical, productive way to plan a menu? By refusing to settle for the same breakfast as yesterday, it keeps me energized with new experience and anticipation, adds a bit of fun to daily life, and above all minimizes wasted ingredients - a planning method and idea-generation technique for a breakfast that's different from yesterday's. I've decided to call this the "chain-reaction breakfast planning methodology." While I was elaborately explaining this great discovery, my coworker M blew her nose and said flatly, "Every homemaker does that." (Na-Josik, Breakfast That Chains Into the Next) Clearing out yesterday's leftover seaweed, about 300kcal per serving, #CornSeaweedColdSoba.

Ingredients

2 servings buckwheat noodles, 1 cup corn, 1 egg, 10g seaweed, crispy chili onion, (dressing) 1.5T tahini sauce, 1.5T miso, 2T soy sauce, 1T vinegar, 2t sesame oil, 1T lime juice, 1 cup water

Steps

1. Mix 1.5T tahini sauce, 1.5T miso, 2T soy sauce, 1T vinegar, 2t sesame oil, and 1T lime juice, then stir in 1 cup water (adjust the water to taste). 2. Boil the buckwheat noodles, adding a splash of cold water to the pot about three times. 3. Pan-fry the corn to use as a topping. 4. Chop the scallion, soak the seaweed, and hard-boil the egg to prepare. 5. Plate the noodles from step 2, pour over the sauce from step 1, top with corn, seaweed, and egg, sprinkle with scallion, and finish with spicy crispy chili onion (or chili oil if you don't have any).
